Tally, Zoho, ERPNext or custom ERP?

A plain guide for Indian MSMEs and manufacturers who are wondering whether it's time to move beyond Tally.

QUICK ANSWERIf you mainly need GST billing, accounts and simple stock, Tally Prime is usually enough. Move to an ERP when orders live on WhatsApp and spreadsheets, stock never matches, you run production or several locations, or nobody can see an order's status. Many MSMEs keep Tally for accounts and add an ERP or a custom system for operations.
Updated 30 Oct 2026 · by Juhi Chavda

One question keeps coming up on X from Indian business owners: "Should I move from Tally to Zoho, ERPNext or something else?" In one thread a steel trader asked exactly that, and the replies split three ways: stay on Tally, jump to SAP once you're big, or try ERPNext, which one mid-sized manufacturer said had worked well for a year (the thread). Zoho also launched its own ERP for Indian businesses in January 2026 (AIM), which added to the confusion. Here's a plain way to decide.

Signs you've outgrown Tally alone

  • Orders come in on WhatsApp and calls, and someone copies them into a spreadsheet.
  • Stock in the system rarely matches what's in the godown.
  • You make things, and nobody can say what's in production right now.
  • You have more than one godown, branch or shop.
  • The owner has to call three people to answer "where is this order?"
  • Dispatch, billing and payments are tracked in different places.
  • Month-end reports take days, not minutes.

If two or three of these sound familiar, it's time to look beyond accounting software. If none do, Tally is probably fine and you don't need to spend on an ERP yet.

When a custom ERP makes sense (and when it doesn't)

It makes sense when your process is your edge and off-the-shelf software forces you to change it, when your team won't use a complicated tool, or when you need it to fit around Tally, WhatsApp and the way your shop floor already works.

It doesn't when you run a standard trading business. A ready-made tool will be cheaper and faster, and that's what I'd tell you to use.

What a small manufacturer's ERP should cover

  1. Orders: quotation, sales order and a status anyone can check.
  2. Inventory: raw material, work in progress and finished goods, by location.
  3. Production: what to make today, who made it, and wastage.
  4. Purchase: reorder levels, purchase orders and supplier follow-up.
  5. Dispatch: challans, e-way bills and delivery status.
  6. Billing: GST invoices, linked to Tally for accounts.
  7. Reports: a one-screen dashboard the owner can check on a phone.

How to choose in five steps

  1. Write down how an order moves today, from enquiry to payment.
  2. Mark the three steps that waste the most time or cause the most mistakes.
  3. Try two options with your own real data for a week, not a sales demo.
  4. Ask your CA how each one will connect to accounts and GST.
  5. Plan training and a cut-over date. Most ERP projects fail on people, not software.

FAQ

Is Tally an ERP?

Tally Prime covers accounting, GST, inventory and basic manufacturing entries very well, and many MSMEs use it as their main system. It is lighter on production planning, sales pipelines, approvals and shop-floor tracking, which is where a full ERP or a custom system comes in.

When should a small business move from Tally to an ERP?

When orders arrive on WhatsApp and live in spreadsheets, stock in the system rarely matches the godown, you run production or more than one location, or the owner has to call three people to know the status of one order.

Can a custom ERP work alongside Tally?

Yes. Tally can exchange data with other software, so many businesses keep Tally for accounts and GST and run orders, stock, production and dispatch in a separate system that sends entries to Tally.

Is ERPNext free?

ERPNext is open source, so the software itself is free to use and host yourself. You still pay for hosting, setup, changes and support, either to a partner or to your own team.

How long does a custom ERP take for an MSME?

A focused system covering a few modules (orders, inventory, invoicing, reports) usually takes 6 to 12 weeks. A clickable prototype first helps the team agree on screens before any coding starts.
