AEO & SEO news, minus the jargon

The search and AI changes that actually affect small businesses and startups, each with a 'what to do' you can act on this week.

QUICK ANSWERLatest: Google AI Mode adds temporary threads that are not saved or used for personalisation (17 Nov); Google publishes a guide to stop your firewall blocking its Google-Agent shopping bot (17 Nov); FBI warns hacked WordPress sites are being used to rank malware in search (16 Nov). Each story below has a plain-English 'what to do'.
Updated 19 Nov 2026 · by Juhi Chavda
Google17 Nov 2026

Google AI Mode adds temporary threads that are not saved or used for personalisation

Google added temporary threads to AI Mode on 17 November 2026. You start one from an icon in the top right corner. Chats in a temporary thread are kept out of your search history and are not used to personalise later answers, and the thread disappears once you close it. Google says it is rolling out worldwide on desktop and mobile browsers, in every language AI Mode supports.

What to do: When you check how AI Mode talks about your business, use a temporary thread so your own past searches don't colour the answer. It gives you a view closer to what a new customer sees. You can also run a quick check with our AI visibility checker.

Sources: Google blog · 9to5Google · TechCrunch

Google17 Nov 2026

Google publishes a guide to stop your firewall blocking its Google-Agent shopping bot

Google has posted a new help page on letting Google-Agent into your website. This is the bot Google uses when its AI agents visit sites and take actions for a user, like checking stock or filling a cart. Google says you can spot it by a user agent that contains "Google-Agent" and an IP address from its published user-triggered-agents list. It also asks sites to skip CAPTCHAs and bot challenges for verified Google-Agent visits, since these can quietly block real customers' requests.

What to do: If you use Cloudflare, a firewall or a bot blocker, ask your developer to add an allow rule for verified Google-Agent traffic and to check that your cart and checkout pages don't throw a CAPTCHA at it.

Sources: Google Search Central: Allow Google-Agent traffic · Search Engine Roundtable

Google16 Nov 2026

FBI warns hacked WordPress sites are being used to rank malware in search

The FBI's FLASH alert of 16 November says a malware group called Gootloader uses hacked websites and SEO tricks to push infected files to people searching on Google. As of October 2026 the FBI counted over 1,000 infected WordPress sites and 216 domains run by the group. It links the scheme to more than $28 million in ransomware payments.

What to do: Update WordPress, your theme and every plugin this week, delete plugins you don't use, and turn on two-factor login for all admin accounts. Check Search Console's Security issues report too.

Sources: FBI / IC3 FLASH alert (PDF) · Search Engine Roundtable

Search Console API now gives branded query, Google Lens and multi-country data

On 16 November 2026 Google said the Search Analytics API now has three filters that were already in the Search Console website. You can split branded and non-branded searches (only for eligible sites), pull web multimodal data from Google Lens, Circle to Search and image uploads, and filter by several countries or devices in one request. Google12 Nov 2026

Google publishes a guide to spotting Google Search visits in your analytics and logs

Google added a new Search Central page that explains how to find visits from Google Search using the referrer your server receives. The referrer shows only the Google domain (for example google.com or google.co.in), never the search words. Google lists why your analytics will not match Search Console: private browsing and ad blockers, redirects on your site that drop the referrer, a click being counted differently from a page visit, and paid traffic mixed in with organic.

What to do: Check that your site does not redirect visitors in a way that strips the referrer (for example http to https to www in several hops). For the search terms people used, look in the Search Console Performance report, not your analytics.

Sources: Google Search Central

Google11 Nov 2026

Google warns that moving to a CDN can flood your server with Googlebot visits

Google published a post and video on 11 November about switching a website to a CDN. A faster site can make Googlebot crawl much more, and a new CDN starts with an empty cache, so every first visit to a page still lands on your main server. Google's bigger worry is what site owners do next: turn on strict bot blocking that also shuts out Googlebot.

What to do: If you are moving to Cloudflare or another CDN, warm the cache first, keep verified Googlebot allowed in your bot rules, and use rate limits instead of blanket blocks. Watch the Crawl stats report in Search Console for the first week.

Sources: Google Search Central Blog · Search Engine Roundtable

Bing30 Oct 2026

Bing lets sites block AI training but still appear in Copilot answers

In a Bing blog post on 30 October 2026, Microsoft split its AI controls in two. AI training can be blocked in robots.txt or per page. AI grounding (using your page to build answers in Copilot, Bing AI Overviews and AI Mode) is set per page only, with a meta tag or header. A middle option, disallowextended, keeps your pages in Microsoft's own AI answers but keeps them out of the grounding tools Microsoft sells to other developers.

Both settings stay on Allow unless you change them. Microsoft starts reading them on 1 February 2027 and says normal Bing rankings are not affected, but warns that blocking grounding will cut your visibility in its AI answers.

What to do: Most small businesses want to be cited, so leave grounding on Allow and only block training if you have a reason to. If you use noarchive or nocache tags today, check Bing Webmaster Tools after 15 December, as those sites get a temporary grounding setting there.

AI search30 Oct 2026

Google, Bing and OpenAI agree on one robots.txt line to opt out of AI training

On 30 October 2026 Google, Microsoft and OpenAI said they will honour a shared robots.txt token, __usage_ai-training, that tells AI companies not to train models on your pages. It comes from an IETF draft, and an Anthropic engineer told the IETF group the company has started adding support too. Rollout is slow: Microsoft says it starts on 1 February 2027, Google says early 2027, and OpenAI says "in the coming months". Google and Microsoft both say the opt-out does not affect normal Search or Bing rankings.

Two details matter. A User-agent: * rule does not count as this opt-out, so you have to name the token. And Google will treat it like blocking Google-Extended, which also stops your pages being used to ground answers in the Gemini app.

What to do: Most businesses that want AI tools to recommend them should not opt out. Publishers who want to protect their writing can add the token now and stay in search. Check or build your file with the free AI crawler robots.txt generator and checker.

Sources: PPC Land · Google Search Central · Search Engine Roundtable
