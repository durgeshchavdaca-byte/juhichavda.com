What this checks

  • MX records: which service receives your email (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Zoho and others).
  • SPF: that you have exactly one SPF record, it ends with ~all or -all, and it stays under the 10 DNS lookup limit.
  • DKIM: that a signing key exists. We try your selector plus 24 common ones used by Google, Microsoft, Zoho, Mailchimp, SendGrid, Brevo and others.
  • DMARC: that a policy exists, what it is set to, and whether you receive reports.
  • BIMI: whether your logo can show next to your emails in supporting inboxes. This is optional.

FAQ

Why are my emails going to spam?

The most common reasons are missing or broken SPF, DKIM or DMARC records, sending from a new domain too fast, a high spam complaint rate, no unsubscribe link, and links or images that look like spam. Start by checking your domain's records above.

What are SPF, DKIM and DMARC?

SPF lists which servers may send email for your domain. DKIM adds a signature that proves the email wasn't changed and really came from you. DMARC tells inboxes what to do when SPF or DKIM fail, and sends you reports. Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook now expect all three from bulk senders.

What are Gmail's bulk sender requirements?

If you send more than 5,000 emails a day to Gmail addresses, Google requires SPF, DKIM and a DMARC record, a From address that aligns with them, one-click unsubscribe for marketing emails, and a spam complaint rate below 0.3%. Google recommends staying under 0.1%. Yahoo and Outlook have similar rules.

How do I find my DKIM selector?

Open an email you sent, choose 'Show original' in Gmail, and look for 's=' inside the DKIM-Signature line. That word is the selector. Google Workspace usually uses 'google', Microsoft 365 uses 'selector1' and 'selector2'.

Is p=none enough for DMARC?

It meets the minimum rule for bulk senders and is the right first step, because you can read reports without blocking anything. Once your reports look clean, move to p=quarantine and later p=reject to stop others sending email that pretends to be you.
